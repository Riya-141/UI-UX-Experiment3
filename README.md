# UI-UX-Experiment3

## Aim:
To apply Heuristic Evaluation to existing apps, and to compare and redesign two competing OTT streaming apps (Netflix and Amazon Prime Video).

## Problem Statement:
Compare and analyze the UX of 2 competing apps from the same domain (OTT video streaming). Develop and incorporate the required changes in the analysed apps, and produce a redesigned app that has something new which is not present in either of them.

## Algorithm:
1. Select two competing apps from the same domain: Netflix and Prime Video.

2. Use Jakob Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics as the evaluation criteria.

3. Explore both apps and observe their home page, account page, subscription page, navigation, search and playback interactions.

4. Identify the strengths, weaknesses and heuristic violations of each app.

5. Compare both apps on usability, clarity, personalization and visual design.

6. Propose improvements and redesign concepts to fix the usability issues.

7. Create a hybrid redesigned interface, "StreamNest", combining the best features of both apps and adding new features.

8. Design three screens in Figma-style: Home, Account and Subscription.

9. Validate the changes using A/B testing by comparing the original apps with the redesigned version.

10. Summarize the results and conclude the improvements in user experience.

## Output:
A heuristic evaluation was conducted on two competing OTT streaming applications: Netflix and Prime Video. These apps were selected because of their huge global user base and overlapping functions such as video streaming, personalized recommendations, watchlists, downloads and subscription plans. The evaluation used Jakob Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics, focusing on visibility of system status, consistency and standards, recognition rather than recall, user control and freedom, flexibility and efficiency of use, aesthetic and minimalist design, and error prevention.

### Comparison and Analysis of User Experience

### Netflix: 
Netflix has the strongest personalization, with rows such as "Continue Watching", "Previews" and "Popular on Netflix". Its dark interface is clean, consistent and cinematic, and the hero banner and thumbnail rows make browsing easy. However, the home page offers limited ways to filter by mood or available time, auto-playing previews can distract users, and plan details are fixed monthly tiers with little flexibility.

### Prime Video: 
Prime Video offers a large catalog and extra benefits bundled with the Prime membership. However, free "included with Prime" titles are mixed with rent or buy titles, which causes confusion, and the home screen can feel cluttered. Subscription and billing information is tied to the wider Prime account, which makes the plan less clear.

### Overall Observation:
Netflix scored highest in personalization, visual consistency and ease of browsing. Prime Video scored highest in bundled value and catalog variety, but lacked clarity in content labelling and a clutter-free interface. Neither app combined all of the following in one design: mood-based discovery, a clean uncluttered interface, flexible pay-as-you-watch billing, a group watching experience with shared billing, and a single watchlist across studios.

### Development and Incorporation of Required Changes:
Based on the heuristic evaluation, a hybrid app named StreamNest was designed with three screens (Home, Account and Subscription).

### Proposed Improvements:

* A cinematic dark interface with a hero banner, Previews row and Popular row inspired by Netflix.
* Bundled perks and a clear "included" label inspired by Prime Video.
* A clutter-free home screen with fewer promotions and more relevant recommendations.
* A modern, consistent icon set and a unified colour palette (midnight navy, violet gradients, coral-orange accents).
* Clear subscription cards with a Monthly/Yearly toggle and a one-tap free trial.

### Unique Add-Ons Not Present in Netflix or Prime Video

* Mood Match: The user picks a mood (Cozy, Thrilled, Curious, Laugh) and available time, and the app suggests a matching title.
* Co-Watch Circles: Users create a group, watch together, and split the subscription bill among members.
* Flex Pass: A weekly plan (₹49/week) where the user pays only for the weeks they watch and can pause anytime.
* Unified watchlist: One watchlist and search across studios.

These solutions aim to increase usability, simplify discovery, reduce subscription friction and give users more control without overwhelming them.

### Evaluation of Design Changes Through A/B Testing
To verify the improvements, A/B testing can be implemented with two user groups. Group A (Control) uses the original Netflix or Prime Video apps. Group B (Test) uses the redesigned StreamNest interface.

### Metrics to compare:

* Average time to choose a title
* Bounce rate from the home screen
* Watchlist and Co-Watch Circle usage
* Subscription completion rate
* Free-trial to paid conversion
* User satisfaction through in-app surveys
* Average daily watch time

### Expected Outcomes

* Faster title selection because of Mood Match.
* Lower bounce rate from the home screen because of clearer recommendations.
* Higher subscription completion because of simpler plans and Flex Pass.
* Higher engagement and retention because of Co-Watch Circles.
* Increased user satisfaction from better control and personalization.

## Result:
The heuristic evaluation of Netflix and Prime Video showed that combining their strengths, while fixing their weaknesses, improves the overall user experience of an OTT app. The redesigned StreamNest app has clearer navigation and better personalization, and it adds new features that neither app offers: Mood Match, Co-Watch Circles and Flex Pass.
